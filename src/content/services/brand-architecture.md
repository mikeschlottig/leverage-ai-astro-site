## What is brand architecture in the AI era?

Brand architecture in the AI era is not a visual identity system. It is an information architecture system — the set of consistent signals that collectively determine how AI systems understand, represent, and recommend your brand.

When AI systems encounter your brand across different sources, they synthesize a description. If those sources are inconsistent, the synthesis is wrong. Your LinkedIn might say you are a "digital marketing agency in Grants Pass." Your website might say you are an "AI search optimization studio serving Oregon." Your Google Business Profile might list a different phone number than your Yelp page. Each of these discrepancies degrades the confidence an AI system has in your entity identity — and low-confidence entities do not get cited.

I approach brand architecture as a technical discipline, not a creative exercise. My goal is to make your brand's entity graph so clean, so consistent, and so well-corroborated that AI systems have no choice but to represent you accurately. One person. Full-stack. AI-multiplied. No account manager in the middle. You talk directly to the person building your infrastructure.

## How do AI systems form opinions about your brand?

AI systems do not have opinions in the human sense, but they do have confidence scores — and those scores functionally determine what gets said about you. When a large language model generates a response that mentions your brand, it is drawing on a weighted combination of training data, retrieved documents, and embedded entity relationships. If the sources it retrieves are contradictory, the model will either omit your brand entirely or describe it in a generic, potentially inaccurate way.

The formation process happens in three stages: entity recognition, entity resolution, and entity synthesis. First, the system must recognize that a string of text refers to a specific business entity and not a common noun or a different organization with a similar name. Second, it must resolve which specific entity you are — linking mentions across sources to a single canonical identity. Third, it must synthesize a description from the most consistent and authoritative signals it can find.

My brand architecture work targets all three stages. I make your brand recognizable by using consistent naming and explicit entity markup. I make it resolvable by building sameAs link architecture that ties every mention to a canonical reference. And I make it synthesizable by ensuring that the sources AI systems trust most — Wikidata, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, and industry directories — all contain accurate, aligned descriptions.

## What does a brand entity audit cover?

I run a systematic brand entity audit that tests how each major AI platform currently describes your brand. This is not a manual spot-check; it is a structured test suite covering dozens of query variations, platform combinations, and contextual prompts.

The audit documents: how each platform describes your industry category, your service area, your key differentiators, and your contact information. It flags hallucinations — instances where an AI system invents facts about your brand that were never true. It identifies omission patterns — queries where you should appear but do not. And it maps competitor mentions — which brands the AI systems cite instead of you, and what signals those competitors have that you lack.

From this baseline, I build an entity description framework: a canonical set of descriptions in multiple lengths and contexts. You get a one-sentence version for directory listings, a one-paragraph version for AI-surfaced overviews, and a full-page version for About pages and press kits. Each version is engineered for semantic consistency — they say the same thing in different lengths, never contradicting each other.

## What is a sameAs link and why does it matter?

The `sameAs` schema property is one of the most underutilized tools in digital marketing, and it is also one of the most powerful for AI entity resolution. It creates explicit, machine-readable links between your website's Organization schema and your brand's records in authoritative external databases.

I implement sameAs architecture systematically. The core targets are Wikidata, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn Company, and Crunchbase. For local businesses, I add industry-specific directories and regional business associations. Each sameAs link acts as a vote of confidence in your entity identity: when four authoritative databases all confirm that "Leverage AI LLC" is the business at this URL with this phone number in this city, AI systems can cite you with high confidence.

Most brands have never implemented sameAs links at all. Some have implemented them incorrectly — linking to profiles that do not exist, or using URLs that redirect to different pages, or mixing personal and company profiles. I audit the entire sameAs graph, fix broken links, add missing targets, and validate that every link resolves to a page that corroborates your core entity data.
