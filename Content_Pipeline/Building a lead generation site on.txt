## 1. The "Ghost Agency" Model (White Label)
You can absolutely run this without putting **LEVERAGEAI LLC** on the frontend. In the lead-gen world, this is often called a "blind" or "unbranded" lead site.
* **The Brand:** The brand *is* the domain. Position the site as an independent directory or a "Contractor Concierge" service (e.g., "The Oregon Roofer Network").
* **Separation:** Keep the site on its own Cloudflare account or at least a separate project. There is no SEO "dilution" risk unless you interlink them with heavy anchor text or share the same physical address/phone number in the schema.
* **Monetization:** You can sell leads in two ways:
    * **Pay-per-Lead:** $50–$150 per qualified call or form fill.
    * **Flat-Fee Rental:** A local roofer pays you $500–$2,000/month to "rent" the site, where all calls and forms go directly to them.

## 2. Leveraging Your GEO Edge
Since you are already deep into **Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)**, this site is the perfect sandbox. AI engines (Gemini, ChatGPT, Perplexity) prioritize "authoritative list mentions" and "third-party validation."
* **The Strategy:** Instead of making it a standard contractor site, build it as a **"High-Authority Directory."** * **The Content:** Create "Top 10 Roofers in [City]" pages for Medford, Grants Pass, Eugene, etc. 
* **The Result:** When someone asks an AI, "Who is the best roofer in Southern Oregon?", your site (oregon-roofer.com) is cited as the authoritative source. You then sell the "top spot" on those lists to your actual clients.

## 3. Tech Stack & Performance
Since you prefer **Astro** and **Cloudflare**, you can build a lightning-fast, edge-native site that crushes the typically slow, bloated WordPress sites of local contractors.
* **Speed as a Signal:** High Core Web Vitals will help you maintain and grow that "natural" traffic you're already seeing.
* **Minimalist UI:** Stick to your preferred card-based, high-contrast layouts. Users looking for a roofer want a "Call Now" button and a "Get a Quote" form, not a complex lifestyle blog.

## 4. Oregon CCB Nuances
In Oregon, the **Construction Contractors Board (CCB)** is strict about advertising. 
* **The Rule:** Anyone "holding themselves out" as a contractor must have a CCB license.
* **The Workaround:** Ensure your site's footer and "About" page explicitly state: *"Oregon-Roofer.com is a referral service and not a licensed contractor. We connect homeowners with pre-screened, licensed Oregon roofing professionals."*
* **Disclaimer:** Since you already own an electrical business, you know the drill—just avoid saying "We do the work" and stick to "We find you the pro."

Advice on the "Conversion" Angle
To satisfy both the homeowner and the contractor simultaneously, keep these three psychological triggers in mind for the build:

Advice: Don't just list companies; label them. Use components like "CCB License Verified" or "Local Rogue Valley Favorite." This builds the "toll booth" authority you're looking for.

2. The "Qualified Lead" Promise (For Contractors)

Advice: Your lead form shouldn't just ask for a phone number. It should ask: Is it a repair or replacement? Shingle or Metal? How many stories is the house? * The Pitch: When you eventually rent this site to a roofer, you tell them: "These aren't just names; these are full project specs from people in your zip code."
