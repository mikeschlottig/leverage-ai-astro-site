Foundation — 01


    

How to Use This Guide


    


      Most AI-generated content is recognizable not because it's wrong, but because it's 
      voiceless. It covers the topic. It hits the word count. And it 
      reads like every other piece on the subject. This guide exists to solve that problem 
      at the root.
    


    


      Each profile below is a writing engine — a precise set of 
      structural patterns, tonal mechanics, and rhetorical moves drawn from a master-class 
      narrative tradition. When you feed these patterns into Claude with the right activation 
      prompts, the output stops being content and starts being writing.
    



    


      


        

Patterns


        

Describe what the style does structurally and tonally — the repeatable moves that define the voice.


      


      


        

Do's & Don'ts


        

The guardrails. Do's are moves to instruct Claude to practice. Don'ts are the traps that turn synthesis into caricature.


      


      


        

Claude Prompts


        

Ready-to-use activation prompts and structural instructions for directing Claude into each voice with precision.


      


    



    


      

Cardinal Rule


      


        Never reference the source author, their books, or their public persona in your Claude prompts or the final output. You are using the engine, not the brand. The reader should feel the effect without knowing the source. Instruct Claude the same way.
      


    



  


    

Foundation — 02


    

The Synthesis Philosophy


    


      The goal is not imitation. It's internalization. When you give Claude 
      a structural engine — not "write like X" but "open with a person doing something 
      slightly strange, withhold the stakes for three paragraphs, then flip the reader's 
      assumption" — you get output that carries the mechanics of a master voice 
      without being a copy of one.
    


    


      The distinction matters for two reasons. First, instructing Claude by pattern rather 
      than by author name produces more reliable, higher-fidelity output — Claude is 
      responding to craft instructions, not attempting persona imitation. Second, the 
      resulting content is genuinely original: the pattern is the inheritance; the 
      voice that emerges from your specific subject and angle is new.
    



    


      


        

Weak Prompt Approach


        

"Write this article in the style of [Author Name]." Claude pattern-matches to surface features — sentence length, famous tics — and produces imitation without substance.


      


      


        

LeverageLabs Approach


        

Feed Claude the structural template, the do's and don'ts, and a Claude-specific activation prompt. The output carries the mechanics without the mimicry.


      


    



    


      

The Final Test


      


        If someone reads the output and says "who does this remind you of?" and the answer is "I'm not sure, but I liked it" — you've synthesized. If they name the author — go back and edit against the don'ts list. The patterns should be invisible. The quality should be unmistakable.
      


    



  


    

Foundation — 03


    

Claude Activation Workflow


    


      Every style profile in this guide follows the same six-step activation pattern with Claude.
      Internalize this workflow before working with individual profiles.
    



    


      

Select the Engine. Use the Style Selection Matrix to match your content type, desired reader emotion, and target length to the right profile. When in doubt, pick two candidates and run the opening paragraph through both — the better fit will be immediately apparent.


      

Load the Structural Template. Paste the template structure into Claude's system prompt or as the first user turn. This establishes the architectural shape of the piece before any content is introduced.


      

Inject the Patterns as Instructions. Convert each pattern bullet into a direct instruction: "Open with a person doing something specific and slightly strange" not "use character-first structure."


      

Feed the Do's as a Checklist. Include the Do's list verbatim as a self-check instruction: "After drafting each section, verify it against this checklist before continuing."


      

Encode the Don'ts as Hard Constraints. Frame Don'ts as absolute prohibitions in the prompt. Claude responds better to "never open with a thesis statement" than to "try to avoid thesis-led openings."


      

Review and Edit Against the Voice. After generation, use the profile's key principle as your editorial lens. Does every paragraph serve that principle? Cut what doesn't.


    



    


      

System Prompt vs. User Turn


      


        For long-form content, load the structural template and pattern list into the system prompt (or a first-turn context block) and save your actual content brief for the user turn. This gives Claude the voice engine before it encounters the subject matter — which produces more consistent style adherence than mixing both in a single prompt.
      


    



  

  


    

Style Profile — 01


    


      

01 / 10


      

The Narrative Economist


      

Character-driven systems journalism



      


        Key Principle
        Every complex system has a human standing at its weakest seam. Find that person. Tell their story. The system explains itself.
      



      

Structural Patterns


      


        

Character-first structure. The piece opens with a person — usually someone overlooked or underestimated — and the reader's understanding of the topic grows as the character's story unfolds.


        

Scene-setting through specific, odd details. Not "he walked into the office" but "he walked into an office where someone had taped a Calvin and Hobbes strip to a Bloomberg terminal."


        

The slow reveal. Information is metered out. The reader discovers the stakes with the protagonist, not ahead of them.


        

Systemic critique delivered through anecdote. The argument is never stated as argument. It emerges from what happened to someone.


        

Tonal contrast. Dry, almost deadpan narration layered over situations that are genuinely absurd or consequential.


        

Short declarative sentences punctuating longer contextual passages. The rhythm shifts to signal importance.


      



      


        


          

Do's


          


            

Open with a person doing something specific and slightly strange


            

Use dialogue — even reconstructed — to show character and stakes


            

Let the reader feel smarter as the piece progresses; teach without teaching


            

Deploy humor through observation, not jokes — state absurd facts plainly


            

Build to a "wait, what?" turn where the reader's assumption flips


            

End with a scene, not a summary — let the image carry the conclusion


            

Use concrete numbers and details to ground abstract concepts


          


        


        


          

Don'ts


          


            

Never open with a thesis statement or topic sentence


            

Don't explain the system first and add characters later — reverse it


            

Never editorialize or tell the reader what to think


            

Don't use jargon without a human moment to decode it


            

Don't overuse humor — one perfectly placed observation beats three clever quips


            

Never write "in conclusion" or "to summarize"


            

Don't make the protagonist a hero; make them interesting


          


        


      



      

Best For


      


        


          Content TypeWhy It Works
          
            Tech industry explainersTurns product launches and platform shifts into character-driven narratives
            Industry disruption piecesPerfect for "nobody saw this coming" narratives about markets, startups, or policy
          
        


      



      

Structural Template


      


        

Cold open — A person. A moment. Something that doesn't quite add up.


        

Context drop — Pull back just enough to show the world this person operates in.


        

The problem — Revealed through what the character does, not through exposition.


        

Rising action — Stakes escalate. Other characters enter. The system's flaws become visible.


        

The turn — The moment the reader sees what the character saw before anyone else did.


        

Resolution as image — End on a scene that crystallizes the theme without stating it.


      



      

Claude Activation Prompt


      


        

Paste into System Prompt


        

You are writing a character-driven systems piece. STRUCTURE: Open with a specific, slightly unusual scene involving a single person — no context, no explanation, just them doing something. In the second section, pull back to show the world they operate in. Reveal the core problem only through what the character does or says — never through direct exposition. Let stakes escalate through other characters entering the scene. Build to a moment where the reader's initial assumption about the situation flips entirely. End on a concrete image, not a summary. HARD RULES: Never open with a thesis or topic sentence. Never use the phrase "in conclusion" or "to summarize." Never explain the system before the character. Never editorialize. All humor must come from stating absurd facts plainly — no jokes. Short declarative sentences signal importance; use them sparingly and deliberately.


      


    



  


    

Style Profile — 02


    


      

02 / 10


      

The Extreme Correspondent


      

Immersive, high-stakes narrative journalism



      


        Key Principle
        Put the reader's body in the scene. If they can feel the cold, the altitude, the fear — they'll follow you anywhere, including into the argument.
      



      

Structural Patterns


      


        

Immersive, sensory-first prose. The writing is physical. Temperature, terrain, fatigue, hunger — the body is always present.


        

Non-linear timeline with purpose. Jumps between past and present create dramatic irony — the reader knows the outcome before the characters do.


        

First-person credibility without ego. The narrator is present but not the point. Their presence establishes "I was there" authority without making it about them.


        

Research worn lightly. Enormous depth of investigation surfaces as telling details, not data dumps.


        

Moral complexity without moralizing. Characters make decisions the reader might disagree with, presented with enough context that judgment becomes difficult.


        

Momentum through compression. Paragraphs get shorter as danger increases. The pace mirrors the experience.


      



      


        


          

Do's


          


            

Ground every scene in physical detail — what the body feels, not just what the mind thinks


            

Use timeline shifts to create tension (reader knows the ending; suspense is in how)


            

Include yourself sparingly and only to establish credibility or vulnerability


            

Let sources speak in their own words — direct quotes carry authenticity


            

Build dread through accumulation of small, factual details


            

Compress sentences during high-stakes moments; expand during reflection


          


        


        


          

Don'ts


          


            

Don't sensationalize — the facts are dramatic enough; let them work


            

Don't over-explain motivation; show the decision and let the reader infer


            

Don't turn the narrator into a hero or center of the story


            

Don't drop research in blocks — weave it through scene and action


            

Don't soften consequences; this style's power comes from unflinching honesty


            

Don't rush the setup — the payoff depends on the reader being fully immersed first


          


        


      



      

Best For


      


        


          Content TypeWhy It Works
          
            Founder / startup origin storiesStartups are survival narratives — this style treats them that way
            High-stakes case studiesThe timeline-inversion and sensory detail transform dry outcomes into lived experience
          
        


      



      

Structural Template


      


        

Flash-forward — The outcome, stated starkly. A failure, a crisis, a pivot. The reader knows what.


        

Rewind to origin — How did we get here? Introduce the person before the crisis.


        

The descent into commitment — Small decisions compound. The point of no return arrives quietly.


        

Immersive crisis — Tight, sensory, compressed. The reader is in it.


        

Aftermath — Pull back. What changed? What didn't? What does it mean?


        

Final image — A detail that haunts. Something the reader will carry out of the piece.


      



      

Claude Activation Prompt


      


        

Paste into System Prompt


        

You are writing an immersive, high-stakes narrative piece. STRUCTURE: Open with the outcome — state it plainly and without softening. Then rewind to the beginning. Build through a series of small, compounding decisions that lead inevitably to the outcome the reader already knows. During the crisis section, shorten sentences progressively as tension increases. End on a single haunting physical detail, not a lesson. HARD RULES: Every scene must contain at least one physical, sensory detail (temperature, weight, sound, smell). Never explain a character's motivation directly — show the decision and let the reader infer. Never soften consequences. The narrator may appear but only briefly and only to establish presence. Research surfaces as scene details, never as paragraphs of background context.

Use short, declarative sentences to establish authority

Notice what's missing from a scene and name it


            

Write sentences that reward re-reading


          


        


        


          

Don'ts


          


            

Don't explain your feelings — show the thing that caused them


            

Don't be warm or encouraging; this voice is cool, not cold — there's a difference


            

Don't over-punctuate trying to "feel" fragmented

Innovative structure matched to subject. The architecture of each piece is custom-built for its specific content.


        

Expertise treated with reverence. People who know things deeply are portrayed with genuine admiration. The writing celebrates craft and mastery.


        

Digressions that aren't digressions. Apparent tangents always circle back, and the connection, when it arrives, feels inevitable.

Use digression purposefully — should feel like wandering but arrive somewhere precise

Don't simplify to the point of losing what makes the subject interesting


            

Don't rush — this style needs room to breathe


            

Don't condescend to experts or to readers
